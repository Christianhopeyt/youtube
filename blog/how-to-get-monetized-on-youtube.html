How to Get Monetized on YouTube in 2026: The Two-Tier Path to Your First Payout

Getting monetized in 2026 is a two-step path: an early-access tier at 500 subscribers that unlocks fan funding, and a full ad-revenue tier at 1,000. Here is exactly what each tier requires and a practical plan to get there.

Getting monetized on YouTube in 2026 is a two-step process, not one big gate. The Partner Program now has an early-access tier at 500 subscribers that turns on fan-funding features, and a full ad-revenue tier at 1,000 subscribers that unlocks advertising. The good news is that only the second tier depends on ad revenue; the first can start paying you months earlier. Here is exactly what each tier needs, how the watch-time math really works, and a plan that gets you from zero to your first payout.

The Two Tiers of the YouTube Partner Program in 2026

YouTube's eligibility system splits into two tiers, and the difference between them is what they unlock. The early-access tier is designed to get small channels earning before they have scale. The full tier adds ad revenue once you prove consistent audience interest. Both tiers use rolling windows, and both sit on top of the same base eligibility requirements.

The two tiers share a logic: prove you can publish consistently and hold an audience, and YouTube will let you earn. The thresholds are the measurable version of that proof, which is why the way to approach them is a content plan, not a counting game.

The Full Ad-Revenue Tier: 1,000 Subscribers

The full tier requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 valid public watch hours in the trailing 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days. Hit either combination and ad revenue unlocks: watch-page ads, Shorts feed ads, and your share of Premium revenue. This is the tier most people mean when they say "monetized", because it is the one that pays for views. Our monetization requirements guide walks through the eligibility rules in more detail.

The Early-Access Tier: 500 Subscribers

The early-access tier needs 500 subscribers, at least three public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months or 3 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days. It unlocks Super Thanks, Channel Memberships, and Shopping, but it does not unlock ad revenue.

That distinction matters for planning. Fan-funding features are often the first real money a small channel sees, and 500 subscribers is a far more reachable target than 1,000. If you are building an audience that cares about your niche, memberships and Super Thanks can start paying while you still grind toward the ad tier. The three-upload requirement is also a useful forcing function: it stops inactive channels from sneaking in, and it keeps the tier honest about channel health.

How Watch Hours Are Counted, and the Traps

Watch hours run on a rolling 12-month window, so hours earned 11 months ago still count today. Only public videos count: private, unlisted, and deleted videos do not, and deleting a video removes the hours it earned. That last rule is the classic trap, a channel that deletes its early, rough videos can quietly erase progress toward the threshold. Keep your early uploads public and simply let them age.

The arithmetic is worth internalizing. Four thousand hours equals 240,000 watch minutes. At an average view duration of four minutes, that is roughly 60,000 quality views to reach the threshold. A channel averaging 300 views a day reaches it in about 200 days; one averaging 1,000 views a day does it in about 60 days. These are illustrative estimates based on the arithmetic, not a guarantee, but they make the scale of the task concrete, and our watch time strategy guide is built around exactly this math.

Quality views beat inflated view counts. Views that bounce after five seconds add almost nothing to the 240,000-minute goal. Retention is the fastest lever on watch hours because it multiplies every view you already earn.

The Shorts Path Uses Views, Not Hours

Shorts watch time does not count toward the 4,000-hour long-form threshold; the Shorts path is measured in views. Ten million valid Shorts views in the last 90 days unlocks the full tier, and three million unlocks early access. The Shorts route is attractive for channels that have not cracked long-form retention, but the 90-day window is demanding: it requires consistent volume, and Shorts revenue is pooled rather than per-video. Our Shorts monetization guide goes deeper into the economics before you commit to that path.

Whichever path you choose, understand that it is a path, not a lottery. Both routes require valid views from public videos, which means the same content quality bar applies. A Short that is watched through and reshared builds toward the view count; a Short that is swiped past in a second does not.

The Requirements That Apply to Every Path

Whatever path you take, the base requirements are identical: an eligible country, a channel in good standing with no active Community Guidelines strikes, two-step verification enabled on your Google Account, and an approved AdSense account linked in Studio. None of these are exotic, but all of them are checked, so handle them before you hit the thresholds rather than after. Two-step verification in particular is easy to put off and painful to discover in the middle of an application.

Review Time: Expect About a Month

Once you meet the thresholds and apply, review typically takes about a month. YouTube checks your channel against the monetization policies and advertiser-friendly guidelines during that window. You do not need to watch the counter; you need to keep uploading content that passes the policies, because repeated strikes during review can pause the process. Treat the review month as a normal publishing month, not a break.

The four-minute math

A 5-8 minute video with a 50% average view duration earns roughly 3 minutes of watch time per view, so it takes about 80,000 such views to hit 4,000 hours. That is why topic selection and packaging matter more than raw upload count: discoverable topics with strong retention shorten the road far more than daily uploads nobody watches.

A Practical Plan to Reach Your First Payout

Thresholds are the what; a plan is the how. The steps below are the practical path most successful channels follow, adapted to the 2026 two-tier system. You will not need all five at once, but skipping the first one makes every other step slower:

  • Choose a niche with real demand and repeatable topics you can sustain for months, validated with public data before you commit.
  • Build a library of 30 to 50 videos in the 5-8 minute range, each targeting a discoverable topic.
  • Optimize titles, thumbnails, and the first 30 seconds so average view duration climbs.
  • Turn on the early-access fan-funding features the moment you pass 500 subscribers.
  • Apply for the full tier as soon as you clear both the subscriber and watch-time or views thresholds.

Step one deserves the emphasis. A niche with demand and repeatable topics means every video you publish compounds toward the threshold instead of starting from zero.
